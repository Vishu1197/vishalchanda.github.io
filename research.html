Research programme

Dengue infects an estimated hundreds of millions of people each year and still has no licensed antiviral. My doctoral work approaches that from three directions at once: find the molecule, understand what the virus is actually doing to the host, and solve the delivery problem that stops most natural product candidates from ever working.

Three strands

What each one is trying to answer

Natural products are the obvious place to look for dengue antivirals and the hardest place to look well. The chemical space is enormous, the assays are slow, and docking scores on their own are a weak filter. The approach I published in 2025 puts a hybrid machine learning model in front of the docking step so that the compounds reaching simulation are already the ones worth simulating, then uses molecular dynamics and binding free energy analysis as the real test of whether a pose survives.

  • Targets across the dengue proteome: the NS2B/NS3 protease, NS5 RNA dependent RNA polymerase and methyltransferase, and the envelope protein
  • Feedforward neural networks, random forest, k-nearest neighbours and ensemble models trained on descriptor and docking data
  • Pocket prediction with P2Rank, docking with AutoDock Vina through UCSF Chimera, all box dimensions measured rather than assumed
  • 100 to 500 ns molecular dynamics in GROMACS with the holo system intact, cofactors and metal ions retained
  • MM-PBSA and MM-GBSA binding free energies, ADMET profiling in ADMETLab 3.0, drug likeness screening
Published 2025 NMAHIB 14(1), 164 Best Oral Presentation 2026

The falling platelet count in severe dengue has long been explained as direct destruction. That explanation does not account for everything the transcriptome shows. This strand of the work reads dengue infected expression data to ask what the non structural proteins are doing to host gene regulatory networks, and which of those changes actually track with disease progression.

  • RNA-Seq and microarray datasets analysed for differential gene expression
  • Pathway enrichment and gene set enrichment analysis to find the programmes being pushed
  • Gene regulatory network reconstruction to separate drivers from passengers
  • Mapping of dengue non structural protein activity onto the affected host pathways
Published 2026 J Thromb Thrombolysis Transcriptomics

A phytochemical with a beautiful binding profile and no bioavailability is not a drug. This strand builds lipid and polymeric nanoparticles to carry antiviral candidates past that wall, characterises what comes out of the formulation, and then tests it against dengue virus serotype 2 in cell culture. A low energy fabrication method for controlling droplet size in gallic acid nano-emulsions came out of this work and is filed as an Indian patent application.

  • Lipid and polymeric nanoparticle formulation for targeted antiviral delivery
  • Low energy fabrication techniques for droplet size control in nano-emulsions
  • Cell culture and virus host interaction studies
  • TCID50, plaque assay and virus neutralisation assays for antiviral efficacy

Method

From library to bench

The pipeline every candidate has to survive before it is worth an assay plate.

Step 01

Library curation

ChEMBL and PubChem identifiers reconciled, descriptors computed
Step 02

Machine learning triage

Hybrid models predict affinity from descriptors, cutting the set before any docking runs
Step 03

Pocket prediction and docking

P2Rank locates the site, the grid box is measured from the lining atoms, AutoDock Vina docks the survivors
Step 04

Molecular dynamics

100 to 500 ns in GROMACS with cofactors and metal ions kept in place, RMSD, RMSF, Rg, hydrogen bonds and SASA
Step 05

Binding free energy and ADMET

MM-PBSA and MM-GBSA rescoring, then drug likeness and toxicity profiling
Step 06

Formulation and assay

Nanoparticle encapsulation, then TCID50, plaque assay and neutralisation against DENV-2

Every step above is backed by an open source tool I wrote and put on GitHub.
